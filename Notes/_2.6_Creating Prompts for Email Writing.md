# UNIT 2 – Fundamental of Prompt Writing

# Topic 6: Creating Prompts for Email Writing

---

## Learning Objectives

After completing this topic, students will be able to:

* Understand how Prompt Engineering can assist with professional email writing.
* Identify the important components of an email-writing prompt.
* Create prompts for formal, professional, academic, and business emails.
* Control email tone, length, audience, and purpose.
* Generate appropriate subject lines.
* Convert informal messages into professional emails.
* Improve poorly written emails using AI.
* Review AI-generated emails for accuracy, tone, and appropriateness.

---

# 1. Introduction

Email is one of the most common forms of professional communication.

Students may need to write emails for:

* Leave requests
* Assignment submissions
* Internship applications
* Job applications
* Meeting requests
* Project communication
* Complaints
* Follow-ups
* Invitations
* Thank-you messages
* Requests for information

AI can help draft these emails, but the quality of the email depends heavily on the instructions given to the AI.

Compare:

> Write an email to my professor.

with:

> Write a polite and professional email to my professor requesting a two-day extension for my assignment because I need additional time to complete testing. Keep it below 150 words and include a clear subject line.

The second prompt provides much more useful context.

---

# 2. Basic Email Structure

A professional email usually contains:

```text
Subject
   ↓
Greeting
   ↓
Opening
   ↓
Purpose
   ↓
Relevant Details
   ↓
Required Action
   ↓
Closing
   ↓
Signature
```

Example:

```text
Subject: Request for Assignment Extension

Dear Professor,

I am writing to request a two-day extension for the
submission of my assignment...

Thank you for considering my request.

Regards,
Pravin
```

---

# 3. Why Prompt Design Matters for Emails

A good email prompt should help AI understand:

* Who is sending the email?
* Who is receiving it?
* Why is it being sent?
* What action is required?
* What information should be included?
* What tone should be used?
* How long should it be?
* What should the subject line be?

---

# 4. Essential Components of an Email Prompt

A practical framework is:

```text id="3z2z2y"
RECIPIENT
    +
PURPOSE
    +
CONTEXT
    +
KEY INFORMATION
    +
TONE
    +
CONSTRAINTS
    +
OUTPUT FORMAT
```

---

# 5. Component 1 – Recipient

Tell AI who will receive the email.

Examples:

* Professor
* HR Manager
* Project Manager
* Client
* College Principal
* Team Leader
* Recruiter
* Friend

Compare:

> Write an email requesting leave.

with:

> Write a formal leave-request email to my department head.

The recipient influences the appropriate tone and level of formality.

---

# 6. Component 2 – Purpose

Clearly state why the email is being written.

Examples:

> Request an assignment extension.

> Apply for an internship.

> Schedule a project meeting.

> Follow up on an interview.

> Report a technical issue.

> Submit a project report.

---

# 7. Component 3 – Context

Context provides the background necessary to write an appropriate email.

Example:

```text id="9ok5ll"
Purpose:
Request assignment extension.

Context:
The assignment is due on Friday.
Most of the implementation is complete, but testing
is taking longer than expected.

Requested extension:
Two days.
```

Now AI has enough information to write a meaningful request.

---

# 8. Component 4 – Key Information

List information that must appear in the email.

For example:

```text id="h9m58m"
Include:
- Assignment name
- Current deadline
- Requested new deadline
- Brief reason
- Polite request
```

This reduces the possibility of missing important details.

---

# 9. Component 5 – Tone

Tone is extremely important in professional communication.

Common tones:

* Formal
* Professional
* Polite
* Friendly
* Persuasive
* Apologetic
* Neutral
* Appreciative

Example:

> Use a formal and respectful tone suitable for communication with a university professor.

---

# 10. Component 6 – Constraints

Examples:

> Keep it under 150 words.

> Do not exaggerate the situation.

> Do not sound demanding.

> Avoid unnecessary details.

> Do not invent information.

---

# 11. Component 7 – Output Format

If you want a complete email, specify:

```text id="g2g7kn"
Output:
Subject:
Email Body:
```

For multiple alternatives:

```text id="t2k1cl"
Provide:
1. Formal version
2. Concise version
3. Friendly-professional version
```

# 22. Example – Converting Informal to Professional

Suppose a student writes:

> Sir I couldn't come today because something came up.
> Please give me the notes.

A prompt can improve it:

```text id="9r8v0c"
Rewrite the following message as a polite and professional
email to a university professor.

Requirements:
- Preserve the original meaning.
- Use respectful language.
- Do not invent a specific reason.
- Keep it concise.

Message:
"""
Sir I couldn't come today because something came up.
Please give me the notes.
"""
```

# 23. Why "Don't Invent Information" Matters

Suppose the student says:

> I was absent due to a personal reason.

AI might generate:

> I was suffering from a severe medical condition.

That information was never provided.

Therefore, prompts should sometimes include:

> **Do not invent or assume personal details.**

# 26. Email Tone Transformation

AI can be used to transform tone.

### Original

> Send me the report immediately.

Prompt:

```text id="q7v8m2"
Rewrite this message as a polite and professional request
to a colleague.

Maintain the original objective but remove demanding language.
```

Possible result:

> Could you please share the report at your earliest convenience?

---

# 27. Tone Comparison

| Tone         | Example                                                                                   |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Casual       | Can you send me the file?                                                                 |
| Polite       | Could you please send me the file?                                                        |
| Professional | Could you please share the requested file at your convenience?                            |
| Formal       | I would appreciate it if you could share the requested file at your earliest convenience. |

Students should learn that **more formal does not always mean better**.

The appropriate tone depends on the relationship and context.

# 28. Email Length Control

Different situations require different lengths.

### Very short

> Meeting confirmation.

### Short

> Simple request.

### Medium

> Internship inquiry.

### Longer

> Complex business proposal.

Instead of:

> Make it short.

Use:

> Keep the email below 120 words.

# 29. Email Personalization

Generic emails can sound robotic.

Prompt:

```text id="s7t5d2"
Write a professional internship inquiry email.

Personalization:
- Mention my interest in software development.
- Mention that I recently completed a Java project.
- Connect my interest to the company's software products.

Do not invent any additional experience.
```

This creates a more relevant message.

---

# 30. Personalization Without Fabrication

Important rule:

> **Personalize only using information that is actually known.**

Do not ask AI to:

> Make me sound like an experienced developer.

if the student has no such experience.

Instead:

> Highlight my academic projects and programming skills professionally without exaggerating my experience.

# 43. Best Practices

### Do

* Clearly identify the recipient.
* State the purpose.
* Provide relevant context.
* List important points.
* Specify the appropriate tone.
* Give a word limit when necessary.
* Request a clear subject line.
* Tell AI not to invent information.
* Review the final email before sending.
* Verify names, dates, attachments, and other details.

### Don't

* Provide unnecessary personal information.
* Ask AI to fabricate excuses.
* Use exaggerated language.
* Blindly send AI-generated emails.
* Use the same tone for every recipient.
* Include confidential information unnecessarily.
* Assume a polished email is automatically factually correct.

---

# 44. Important Privacy Practice

Students should be careful when using AI to draft emails.

Avoid unnecessarily sharing:

* Passwords
* Financial information
* Private credentials
* Confidential company information
* Sensitive personal information
* Private information about other people

Instead, use placeholders:

```text
[COMPANY NAME]
[PROJECT NAME]
[CLIENT NAME]
[ORDER NUMBER]
```

# 45. Common Mistakes

| Mistake            | Better Approach                   |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------- |
| "Write an email"   | Define recipient and purpose      |
| No tone            | Specify appropriate tone          |
| No context         | Provide relevant background       |
| Too long           | Define word limit                 |
| Too formal         | Match tone to recipient           |
| Generic content    | Provide relevant details          |
| Fabricated excuses | Tell AI not to invent information |
| Weak subject       | Request a descriptive subject     |
| No review          | Proofread before sending          |

# 50. Quick Revision

## Remember:

> **R + P + C + K + T + C + O**

```text
R → Recipient
P → Purpose
C → Context
K → Key Points
T → Tone
C → Constraints
O → Output
```

A good email prompt tells AI:

> **Who → Why → Background → What to include → How to sound → What limits → What format**

---

# Summary

Prompt Engineering can make email writing faster, clearer, and more consistent. A well-designed email prompt should provide the **recipient, purpose, relevant context, key information, appropriate tone, constraints, and desired output format**.

Students should also understand that professional communication requires human judgment. AI can draft an email, but the sender remains responsible for checking **names, dates, facts, attachments, commitments, tone, and confidentiality** before sending it.

The goal is not simply:

> **"Ask AI to write an email."**

The better approach is:

> **"Give AI the right communication context so it can help draft the right message for the right person in the right tone."**
