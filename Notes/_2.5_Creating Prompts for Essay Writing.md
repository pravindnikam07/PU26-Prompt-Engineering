# UNIT 2 – Fundamental of Prompt Writing

# Topic 5: Creating Prompts for Essay Writing

---

## Learning Objectives

After completing this topic, students will be able to:

* Understand how AI can be used for essay writing.
* Identify the essential components of an essay-writing prompt.
* Create prompts for different types of essays.
* Control essay length, tone, audience, structure, and complexity.
* Generate an essay outline before writing the complete essay.
* Improve weak essay prompts.
* Use AI for brainstorming, drafting, editing, and reviewing essays.
* Avoid common problems such as fabricated facts and overly generic content.

---

# 1. Introduction

An essay is a structured piece of writing that presents ideas, arguments, information, or opinions about a particular topic.

AI can assist with many stages of essay writing:

```text id="v9bqfr"
Topic Selection
      ↓
Brainstorming
      ↓
Research Planning
      ↓
Outline
      ↓
Draft
      ↓
Review
      ↓
Revision
      ↓
Final Essay
```

However, students should understand:

> **AI should be used as a writing assistant, not as a substitute for thinking, learning, or academic responsibility.**

---

# 2. Why Prompt Design Matters in Essay Writing

Consider:

> Write an essay on AI.

The AI does not know:

* Who will read it?
* How long should it be?
* What type of essay?
* What tone?
* What points should be covered?
* Should examples be included?
* Should arguments be balanced?
* What structure is expected?

A better prompt provides these requirements.

# 4. Basic Essay Prompt Template

```text id="rjv1v9"
Act as an experienced academic writer.

Write an essay on [TOPIC].

Audience:
[AUDIENCE]

Purpose:
[PURPOSE]

Length:
Approximately [WORD COUNT] words.

Requirements:
- Include a clear introduction.
- Develop the main ideas logically.
- Include relevant examples.
- Present a clear conclusion.
- Use [TONE] language.

Output Format:
1. Title
2. Introduction
3. Main Body
4. Conclusion
```

---

# 5. Example – Basic Essay Prompt

### Weak Prompt

> Write an essay on Artificial Intelligence.

### Improved Prompt

> Write a 1,000-word essay on Artificial Intelligence for undergraduate students. Explain its major applications, benefits, limitations, and future implications. Use a formal academic tone and structure the essay with an introduction, main body, and conclusion.

### Stronger Prompt

```text
Act as an academic writing instructor.

Write an approximately 1,000-word essay titled:
"The Impact of Artificial Intelligence on Modern Society."

Audience:
Undergraduate engineering students.

Requirements:
- Explain the concept of AI briefly.
- Discuss major applications.
- Explain benefits and potential risks.
- Include relevant real-world examples.
- Present a balanced discussion.
- Avoid unsupported statistics.
- Use clear academic language.
- End with a concise conclusion.

Output Format:
Title
Introduction
Main Discussion
Benefits
Challenges
Future Implications
Conclusion
```

# 6. Important Components

## 6.1 Topic

Clearly identify what the essay should discuss.

Poor:

> Technology.

Better:

> The impact of social media on college students.

---

## 6.2 Audience

Tell AI who will read the essay.

Examples:

* School students
* College students
* Engineering students
* Researchers
* General public

Compare:

> Explain quantum computing.

with:

> Explain quantum computing for first-year engineering students with no prior knowledge of quantum mechanics.

# 7. Purpose

The purpose determines the type of writing.

Possible purposes:

* Inform
* Explain
* Persuade
* Analyze
* Compare
* Discuss
* Critique
* Reflect

Example:

> Write an essay **persuading** college students to adopt responsible AI practices.

This is different from:

> Write an essay **explaining** responsible AI practices.

---

# 8. Essay Type

Students should specify the type of essay when it matters.

Common types include:

### 1. Descriptive Essay

Describes a person, place, event, or subject.

### 2. Narrative Essay

Tells a story or experience.

### 3. Expository Essay

Explains or informs.

### 4. Argumentative Essay

Presents a position supported by reasoning and evidence.

### 5. Compare-and-Contrast Essay

Examines similarities and differences.

### 6. Analytical Essay

Breaks a topic into components and examines relationships.

---

# 9. Example – Descriptive Essay

```text
Write a 700-word descriptive essay about a traditional
Indian festival.

Audience:
Undergraduate students.

Requirements:
- Describe the atmosphere, traditions, food, and cultural
  significance.
- Use vivid but appropriate language.
- Maintain a clear structure.
```

---

# 10. Example – Narrative Essay

```text
Write a 1,000-word narrative essay about a student who
overcomes failure in an important examination.

Requirements:
- Use first-person perspective.
- Include a clear beginning, conflict, turning point,
  and resolution.
- Maintain an encouraging tone.
```

---

# 11. Example – Expository Essay

```text
Act as an academic writer.

Write an 800-word expository essay explaining how
Generative AI works at a high level.

Audience:
First-year Computer Science students.

Requirements:
- Explain the basic concept.
- Explain training and generation at a high level.
- Avoid unnecessary mathematical detail.
- Include a simple analogy.
```

# 12. Example – Argumentative Essay

Argumentative essays require especially careful prompting.

```text
Act as an academic writing instructor.

Write an approximately 1,200-word argumentative essay on:

"Should Generative AI be permitted in university education?"

Requirements:
- Clearly state a thesis.
- Present arguments supporting the position.
- Present significant counterarguments.
- Respond to the counterarguments.
- Use logical reasoning.
- Distinguish facts from opinions.
- Avoid fabricated statistics or citations.
- End with a balanced conclusion.

Audience:
University students and faculty.
```

# 15. Control the Tone

Tone describes the style or attitude of writing.

Examples:

* Formal
* Academic
* Professional
* Conversational
* Persuasive
* Neutral
* Reflective
* Informative

Compare:

> Write an essay about cybersecurity.

with:

> Write a formal, academic essay about cybersecurity for undergraduate students.

---

# 16. Control the Difficulty Level

The same topic can be written at different levels.

### Beginner

> Explain blockchain in simple language for a high-school student.

### Intermediate

> Explain blockchain for undergraduate Computer Science students.

### Advanced

> Provide a technically detailed discussion of blockchain consensus mechanisms for graduate-level Computer Science students.

---

# 17. Control the Length

Instead of:

> Make it short.

Use:

> Write approximately 500 words.

or:

> Keep the essay between 800 and 1,000 words.

However, word-count instructions should be treated as **targets rather than guaranteed exact limits**, because model outputs can vary.

---

# 18. Request an Outline First

For longer essays, it can be useful to generate an outline before the full draft.

### Prompt

```text
Create a detailed outline for a 1,500-word essay on
"The Future of Artificial Intelligence."

Include:
1. Thesis statement
2. Introduction
3. Four main sections
4. Key arguments for each section
5. Counterarguments
6. Conclusion

Do not write the full essay yet.
```

This allows the student to review the structure before generating the complete draft.

---

# 19. Why Outline First?

Consider:

```text
Topic
 ↓
Full Essay
 ↓
Problem discovered
 ↓
Rewrite entire essay
```

versus:

```text
Topic
 ↓
Outline
 ↓
Review
 ↓
Modify
 ↓
Full Essay
```

The second approach provides greater control.

---

# 20. Prompt Chaining for Essay Writing

A complex essay can be divided into multiple prompts.

### Step 1 – Brainstorm

> Generate 10 possible arguments about the impact of AI on education.

### Step 2 – Select

> Evaluate these arguments and identify the five strongest ones.

### Step 3 – Outline

> Create an essay outline using these five arguments.

### Step 4 – Draft

> Write the essay based on the approved outline.

### Step 5 – Review

> Review the draft for logical consistency and unsupported claims.

### Step 6 – Edit

> Improve grammar and clarity without changing the arguments.

This approach introduces **prompt chaining**, which students will study in Unit 4.

---

# 21. Avoid Asking AI to "Make It Human"

Students often write:

> Make this essay sound human.

This is vague.

Better:

> Rewrite the essay using natural, clear language with varied sentence structures while preserving the original meaning.

Even better:

> Improve readability by varying sentence length, reducing repetitive phrasing, and replacing unnecessarily complex expressions with natural academic language.

---

# 22. Avoid Unnecessary "Fancy" Language

A common problem with AI-generated essays is excessive vocabulary.

Prompt:

> Use unnecessarily complex academic vocabulary.

❌ Usually undesirable.

Better:

> Use precise academic language that remains clear and readable for undergraduate students.

---

# 23. Ask AI to Preserve Meaning

When editing an existing essay:

```text
Rewrite the following essay for clarity and grammar.

Requirements:
- Preserve the original meaning.
- Do not add new arguments.
- Do not remove important ideas.
- Do not invent facts.
```

This helps prevent unwanted changes.

# 26. Thesis Statement

AI can help generate possible thesis statements.

Example:

- Keep it specific.
- Make it debatable.

# 30. Essay Fact-Checking Prompt

AI should not be treated as the final authority for factual verification.

A useful review prompt is:

```text
Analyze the following essay for claims that may require
fact-checking.

For each potentially verifiable claim, identify:
1. The claim
2. Why verification may be needed
3. What type of reliable source would be appropriate

Do not assume that a claim is true simply because it appears
in the essay.
```

For important academic work, students should verify claims using **reliable primary or authoritative sources**, according to their institution's requirements.

---

# 31. Citation Warning

Students must understand:

> **Never assume AI-generated citations or references are genuine.**

AI systems can sometimes produce plausible-looking but nonexistent references.

If an essay requires citations:

1. Obtain sources from reliable databases or websites.
2. Verify that every source actually exists.
3. Verify that the source supports the claim.
4. Follow the required citation style.

---

# 32. Academic Integrity

Students should follow their institution's rules regarding AI use.

AI may be useful for:

* Brainstorming
* Understanding concepts
* Creating outlines
* Improving grammar
* Receiving feedback
* Practicing writing

But students should not automatically submit AI-generated work as their own if that violates academic rules.

A good principle is:

> **Use AI to improve your thinking and writing, not to bypass learning.**

# 42. Best Practices

### 1. Start with the objective

Know whether the essay should **explain, analyze, persuade, compare, or describe**.

### 2. Define the audience

Writing for a school student is different from writing for a researcher.

### 3. Specify the essay type

This influences structure and reasoning.

### 4. Generate an outline first for complex essays

It gives better control over the final structure.

### 5. Define the tone

Use terms such as:

> Formal, academic, persuasive, neutral, reflective.

### 6. Give measurable requirements

Instead of:

> Include enough examples.

Use:

> Include three relevant examples.

### 7. Verify factual claims

Especially statistics, historical claims, scientific information, and references.

### 8. Refine instead of regenerating blindly

Identify the problem and modify the prompt accordingly.

# Summary

AI can support essay writing throughout the process—from **brainstorming and outlining to drafting, reviewing, and editing**. The quality of the result depends heavily on how clearly the task is defined.

An effective essay prompt should specify the **topic, purpose, audience, essay type, length, content requirements, tone, and desired structure** when these details matter. For longer or more complex essays, students should consider generating an outline first and then refining the work through multiple stages.

Most importantly, AI-generated essays should not be accepted blindly. **Facts, statistics, citations, and references must be verified**, and students should follow their institution's academic-integrity policies.

> **The best use of AI in essay writing is not "Write my essay." It is "Help me think, plan, write, evaluate, and improve."**
