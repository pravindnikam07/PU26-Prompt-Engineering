# UNIT 2 – Fundamental of Prompt Writing

# Topic 9: Creating Prompts for Story Writing

---

## Learning Objectives

After completing this topic, students will be able to:

* Understand how Prompt Engineering can be used for creative story writing.
* Identify the major components of a story-writing prompt.
* Create prompts for different types of stories.
* Control characters, setting, plot, conflict, tone, and writing style.
* Generate story outlines before writing the complete story.
* Use AI for brainstorming, character development, and story improvement.
* Refine stories using iterative prompting.
* Avoid common problems such as inconsistent characters, predictable plots, and unwanted assumptions.

---

# 1. Introduction

A **story** is a sequence of events involving characters, a setting, a conflict, and some form of resolution or transformation.

A simple story structure is:

```text id="9kq3h2"
Characters
    ↓
Setting
    ↓
Goal
    ↓
Conflict
    ↓
Events
    ↓
Climax
    ↓
Resolution
```

Prompt Engineering can help writers develop:

* Story ideas
* Characters
* Settings
* Plot structures
* Dialogue
* Scenes
* Titles
* Endings
* Alternative versions
* Editing and feedback

---

# 2. Why Story Prompts Are Different

Creative writing is different from tasks such as:

> "Summarize this paragraph."

A story requires many interconnected decisions.

For example:

* Who is the protagonist?
* What does the protagonist want?
* What prevents them from achieving it?
* Where does the story happen?
* What is the emotional tone?
* How does the story end?

Therefore, a simple prompt such as:

> Write a story about a student.

gives AI too much freedom.

---

# 3. Basic Story Prompt Structure

A useful framework is:

```text id="6ct1nz"
ROLE
  ↓
GENRE
  ↓
CHARACTERS
  ↓
SETTING
  ↓
PLOT / CONFLICT
  ↓
THEME
  ↓
TONE
  ↓
LENGTH
  ↓
STYLE
  ↓
ENDING
```

Not every story requires every component.

---

# 4. Component 1 – Role

Tell AI what kind of writer it should emulate functionally.

Examples:

> Act as a creative fiction writer.

> Act as a children's story writer.

> Act as a science-fiction storyteller.

> Act as a screenplay writer.

The role establishes the intended perspective and style of the task.

---

# 5. Component 2 – Genre

Specify the type of story.

Examples:

* Mystery
* Science fiction
* Fantasy
* Horror
* Adventure
* Romance
* Historical fiction
* Comedy
* Thriller
* Slice of life
* Moral story

Compare:

> Write a story about a robot.

with:

> Write a science-fiction mystery about a robot that discovers an unexplained signal.

The second creates a stronger creative direction.

---

# 6. Component 3 – Characters

Define important characters.

Example:

```text id="0v2gq6"
Protagonist:
A 19-year-old engineering student named Aarav.

Personality:
Curious, analytical, slightly introverted.

Goal:
Discover the source of a mysterious signal.

Weakness:
He tends to work alone and distrust others.

Supporting Character:
His classmate Meera, who is creative and socially confident.
```

Character details can help maintain consistency.

---

# 7. Component 4 – Setting

The setting establishes **where and when** the story happens.

Examples:

* A college campus in 2026
* A village in medieval India
* A space station in the year 2150
* A city during a power outage
* A remote mountain laboratory

Example:

> Set the story in a university campus during a heavy monsoon night.

---

# 8. Component 5 – Goal

A protagonist usually needs a meaningful objective.

Examples:

> Find a missing friend.

> Prevent a disaster.

> Win a competition.

> Discover the truth.

> Escape from a dangerous situation.

A clear goal helps create a focused plot.

---

# 9. Component 6 – Conflict

Conflict creates tension.

Common types:

### Person vs Person

Two characters have opposing goals.

### Person vs Nature

The character struggles against environmental forces.

### Person vs Technology

The character faces a technological problem or system.

### Person vs Society

The character challenges social expectations.

### Person vs Self

The character struggles with internal fear, doubt, or conflict.

---

# 10. Example of Conflict

Instead of:

> A student builds an AI system.

Add conflict:

> A student develops an AI system that can predict equipment failures, but the university administration refuses to trust it after its first prediction appears to be wrong.

Now the story has a problem that must be resolved.

---

# 11. Component 7 – Theme

A theme is the underlying idea or message of the story.

Examples:

* Friendship
* Courage
* Responsibility
* Trust
* Technology and humanity
* Perseverance
* Consequences of greed
* Importance of honesty

Example:

> Theme: Technology should support human judgment rather than replace it.

---

# 12. Component 8 – Tone

Tone controls the emotional character of the writing.

Examples:

* Humorous
* Suspenseful
* Emotional
* Dark
* Hopeful
* Mysterious
* Inspirational
* Light-hearted
* Serious

Compare:

> Write a story about a lost phone.

with:

> Write a humorous story about a college student desperately searching for a lost phone during an important presentation.

---

# 13. Component 9 – Length

Instead of:

> Write a long story.

Use:

> Write approximately 1,500 words.

or:

> Write a short story of approximately 800 words.

Word counts are targets rather than guaranteed exact limits.

---

# 14. Component 10 – Style

Students can specify characteristics of the desired writing style without relying on vague instructions.

For example:

> Use vivid descriptions, natural dialogue, short paragraphs, and varied sentence lengths.

Instead of:

> Make it very good.

---

# 16. Example – Mystery Story

```text id="d3z7k1"
Act as a creative mystery-fiction writer.

Write a short mystery story titled:
"The Last Message."

Setting:
A university campus during a rainy evening.

Protagonist:
A Computer Science student who enjoys solving puzzles.

Premise:
The protagonist receives a message from a phone number
that supposedly belongs to a student who disappeared five
years earlier.

Requirements:
- Build suspense gradually.
- Include three possible suspects.
- Provide meaningful clues.
- Include one major twist.
- Make the final explanation logically consistent.
- Do not reveal the culprit early.

Tone:
Mysterious and suspenseful.

Length:
Approximately 1,200 words.
```

# 17. Example – Science Fiction

```text id="3s7j2c"
Act as a science-fiction writer.

Write a short story about an AI system that begins asking
questions about its own purpose.

Setting:
A research laboratory in 2045.

Main Character:
Dr. Maya Rao, an AI researcher.

Conflict:
The AI discovers that its original training data contains
information about an event that has not happened yet.

Theme:
The relationship between technological progress and
human responsibility.

Tone:
Thought-provoking and suspenseful.

Length:
Approximately 1,500 words.

Avoid:
- Excessive technical jargon.
- An instant explanation of the mystery.
```

# 18. Example – Moral Story

```text id="n8q4w5"
Write a short moral story for high-school students.

Theme:
Honesty.

Characters:
A student, a teacher, and two classmates.

Plot:
A student accidentally discovers a mistake in an examination
system that could give them an unfair advantage.

Requirements:
- Present a meaningful ethical dilemma.
- Show the consequences of the decision.
- Avoid preaching excessively.
- End with a clear but natural lesson.

Length:
Approximately 700 words.
```

# 19. Example – Adventure Story

Characters:

Requirements:

# 20. Character Creation Prompt

Sometimes students should create characters before writing the story.

```text id="g8k3v2"
Create three fictional characters for a science-fiction story.

For each character provide:
- Name
- Age
- Occupation
- Personality
- Strengths
- Weaknesses
- Motivation
- Fear
- Relationship with other characters

Ensure that each character is distinct and has a meaningful

```

# 21. Character Consistency

A common problem with AI-generated stories is that characters may change personality or behavior unexpectedly.

For example:

> A character is introduced as shy but suddenly becomes extremely confident without explanation.

A useful instruction is:

```text id="u4m9p7"
Maintain consistent personalities, motivations, relationships,
and character traits throughout the story.

If a character changes significantly, show the development
through events rather than changing the personality suddenly.
```

# 23. Plot Outline Prompt

Before generating the complete story:

```text id="v7p4n2"
Create a detailed outline for a 1,500-word mystery story.

Include:

1. Opening situation
2. Inciting incident
3. First major complication
4. Rising tension
5. Major clue
6. Turning point
7. Climax
8. Resolution

Do not write the full story yet.
```

---

# 24. Why Generate an Outline First?

Without an outline:

```text id="6j0qpb"
Idea
 ↓
AI starts writing
 ↓
Plot develops unpredictably
 ↓
Ending becomes rushed
```

With an outline:

```text id="c3p8y4"
Idea
 ↓
Plot Outline
 ↓
Review
 ↓
Modify
 ↓
Story
```

The second approach gives the writer greater control.

# 25. Scene Generation

A story can be divided into scenes.

Example:

```text id="w3t9q1"
Scene 1 → Introduce protagonist
Scene 2 → Discover mystery
Scene 3 → Investigate
Scene 4 → Major obstacle
Scene 5 → Discovery
Scene 6 → Climax
Scene 7 → Resolution
```

Prompt:

```text id="v1q8s5"
Write Scene 3 of the story.

Purpose of scene:
The protagonist investigates the abandoned laboratory.

Requirements:
- Continue directly from Scene 2.
- Maintain character personalities.
- Introduce one important clue.
- Increase suspense.
- Do not reveal the final mystery.
- End with a reason to continue to the next scene.
```

---

# 26. Dialogue Generation

Dialogue should serve a purpose.

```text id="n7c5x4"
Write a dialogue between two characters who disagree about
whether an AI system should be trusted.

Characters:
Maya – AI researcher who trusts the system.
Arjun – Security engineer who is skeptical.

Requirements:
- Give each character a distinct viewpoint.
- Avoid repeating the same argument.
- Use natural conversational language.
- Gradually increase tension.
- Do not resolve the disagreement yet.
```

---

# 27. Story Ending Prompt

If the ending is weak:

```text id="m5q7d2"
Suggest three possible endings for this story.

For each ending:
- Explain the main idea.
- Maintain consistency with the existing characters.
- Resolve the central conflict.
- Avoid introducing a completely new problem.

Do not rewrite the entire story.
```

Then select one ending.

---

# 28. Twist Generation

```text id="x4k6m9"
Suggest five possible plot twists for this mystery story.

Requirements:
- Each twist must be supported by clues that could appear
  earlier in the story.
- Avoid random or unexplained twists.
- Do not reveal the twist directly in the story yet.

For each twist explain:
1. Twist
2. Supporting clue
3. Effect on the story
```

This teaches students an important principle:

> **A good twist should feel surprising but logical in hindsight.**

# 32. Example – Complete Story Prompt

```text id="p6z2n4"
ROLE:
Act as a professional science-fiction fiction writer.

GENRE:
Science fiction / mystery

TITLE:
"The Signal"

SETTING:
A university research laboratory in India in the year 2040.

MAIN CHARACTER:
A 21-year-old Computer Science student named Riya.
She is analytical, curious, independent, and sometimes
overconfident.

SUPPORTING CHARACTER:
Kabir, her classmate, who is cautious and practical.

PLOT:
Riya discovers an unexplained signal while testing an
experimental AI system.

CONFLICT:
The signal appears to contain information about an event
that has not happened yet.

THEME:
Human responsibility in the development of AI.

REQUIREMENTS:
- Introduce the characters naturally.
- Build suspense gradually.
- Include meaningful clues.
- Use realistic dialogue.
- Maintain character consistency.
- Avoid excessive technical jargon.
- Resolve the central mystery logically.
- Do not introduce supernatural explanations.

TONE:
Suspenseful and thought-provoking.

LENGTH:
Approximately 1,500 words.

STRUCTURE:
Beginning → Conflict → Investigation → Turning Point →
Climax → Resolution
```

# 33. Story Prompt Optimization

Suppose the first output is:

> Too predictable.

Instead of:

> Make it better.

Use:

```text id="y6c3w9"
Revise the plot to make it less predictable.

Requirements:
- Keep the protagonist and setting unchanged.
- Introduce one unexpected but logically supported complication.
- Add at least two clues before the reveal.
- Avoid common "it was all a dream" endings.
- Preserve the central theme.
```

This is **prompt refinement**.

# 34. Iterative Story Development

A useful workflow:

```text id="p8d1k4"
Generate
   ↓
Evaluate
   ↓
Identify Problem
   ↓
Modify Prompt
   ↓
Regenerate
   ↓
Compare
```

Students should learn:

> **Do not simply regenerate. Diagnose the problem first.**

# 41. Common Problems in AI Story Writing

### 1. Predictable plot

**Solution:** Introduce meaningful constraints and ask for alternative plot possibilities.

### 2. Character inconsistency

**Solution:** Define character traits and explicitly request consistency.

### 3. Repetition

**Solution:** Ask AI to identify and reduce repeated ideas or descriptions.

### 4. Weak ending

**Solution:** Develop the ending separately or create multiple alternatives.

### 5. Too many characters

**Solution:** Specify the required main and supporting characters.

### 6. Rushed pacing

**Solution:** Provide a scene-by-scene outline.

### 7. Excessive clichés

**Solution:** Explicitly identify clichés to avoid and request original but coherent alternatives.

---

# 42. Best Practices

### Do

* Define the genre.
* Give characters clear motivations.
* Establish the setting.
* Define the central conflict.
* Specify the desired tone.
* Create an outline for complex stories.
* Develop long stories scene by scene.
* Review character consistency.
* Refine prompts based on specific problems.

### Don't

* Give contradictory character instructions.
* Ask for "the best story" without criteria.
* Add too many unrelated requirements.
* Accept the first output automatically.
* Let AI invent personal experiences that are supposed to be yours.
* Copy generated stories blindly for academic submissions.

---

# 43. Academic Integrity and Creative Writing

If a teacher asks students to write an original story, students should follow the institution's rules regarding AI assistance.

AI can be used for:

* Brainstorming
* Idea generation
* Character development
* Plot alternatives
* Grammar feedback
* Structural feedback

But students should understand the difference between:

> **Using AI as a creative assistant**

and

> **Submitting AI-generated work as entirely their own when that is prohibited.**

# Summary

Prompt Engineering gives writers greater control over AI-assisted creative writing. Instead of simply asking:

> **"Write a story."**

students can define **genre, characters, setting, goals, conflict, theme, tone, length, style, and ending requirements**.

For complex stories, the most effective approach is usually **planning before generation**: develop characters and plot, create an outline, generate scenes, review the draft, and then refine specific weaknesses.

The most important lesson is:

> **A creative prompt should provide direction without removing creativity.**

The goal is not to control every sentence the AI writes. The goal is to provide enough **creative constraints** to guide the story toward the intended result while leaving room for meaningful and unexpected ideas.
