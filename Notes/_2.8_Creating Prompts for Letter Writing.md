# UNIT 2 – Fundamental of Prompt Writing

# Topic 8: Creating Prompts for Letter Writing

---

## Learning Objectives

After completing this topic, students will be able to:

* Understand the purpose and structure of different types of letters.
* Differentiate between formal, informal, and semi-formal letters.
* Create effective prompts for letter writing.
* Control the tone, audience, purpose, length, and format of a letter.
* Use AI to draft, revise, translate, and proofread letters.
* Improve poorly written letter prompts.
* Identify information that should not be fabricated by AI.

---

# 1. Introduction

A **letter** is a written form of communication used to convey information, requests, opinions, feelings, or official communication to another person or organization.

Letters can be:

* Formal
* Informal
* Semi-formal

Although email and messaging applications are now widely used, formal letters remain important in:

* Government communication
* Educational institutions
* Job applications
* Complaints
* Permission requests
* Official applications
* Business communication
* Recommendation requests

AI can assist in drafting letters, but the user must provide sufficient context.

---

# 2. Letter vs Email

Students often confuse letters and emails.

| Letter                                        | Email                                |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Usually follows a traditional document format | Uses email-specific structure        |
| May include sender/receiver addresses         | Usually includes email addresses     |
| Often printed or formally submitted           | Usually digital                      |
| Date and address are commonly included        | Subject line is important            |
| Formal language is common                     | Tone can range from formal to casual |

The **communication purpose** may be similar, but the format is different.

---

# 3. Types of Letters

## 3.1 Formal Letter

Written for official or professional purposes.

Examples:

* Complaint letter
* Application letter
* Permission request
* Letter to an authority
* Job application
* Business letter

---

## 3.2 Informal Letter

Written to friends, family members, or people with whom the writer has a personal relationship.

Examples:

* Letter to a friend
* Invitation to a family member
* Sharing an experience

---

## 3.3 Semi-Formal Letter

Written to someone the writer knows but where a respectful tone is still required.

Examples:

* Letter to a teacher
* Letter to a landlord
* Letter to a colleague
* Request to a known organization

---

# 4. Basic Structure of a Formal Letter

A common formal-letter structure is:

```text
Sender's Address
        ↓
Date
        ↓
Recipient's Address
        ↓
Subject
        ↓
Salutation
        ↓
Introduction
        ↓
Main Body
        ↓
Request / Action
        ↓
Closing
        ↓
Signature / Name
```

The exact format can vary depending on the institution, country, or purpose.

---

# 5. Why Prompt Design Matters

Consider:

> Write a formal letter.

This is insufficient.

AI does not know:

* Who is writing?
* Who will receive it?
* Why is it being written?
* What happened?
* What action is required?
* What tone is appropriate?
* What format should be used?

A good prompt supplies these details.

---

# 6. Letter Prompt Framework

A useful framework is:

```text
ROLE
   ↓
RECIPIENT
   ↓
PURPOSE
   ↓
CONTEXT
   ↓
KEY POINTS
   ↓
TONE
   ↓
FORMAT
   ↓
CONSTRAINTS
```

---

# 7. Component 1 – Recipient

Identify the person or organization.

Examples:

* Principal
* Department Head
* Municipal Officer
* Company Manager
* Bank Manager
* Editor
* Customer Service Department

Example:

> Write a formal complaint letter to the customer service manager of a telecommunications company.

---

# 8. Component 2 – Purpose

Clearly state why the letter is being written.

Examples:

* Request permission
* Make a complaint
* Apply for a position
* Request information
* Report a problem
* Invite someone
* Provide feedback
* Request a certificate

---

# 9. Component 3 – Context

Provide relevant background.

Example:

```text
Problem:
Internet service has been unavailable for three days.

Customer:
Existing broadband customer.

Expected action:
Request investigation and restoration of service.
```

This produces a much more specific letter than:

> Write a complaint letter about internet service.

---

# 10. Component 4 – Key Points

Tell AI exactly what must be included.

Example:

```text
Include:
- Date the problem started
- Description of the issue
- Previous complaint reference
- Expected resolution
- Request for an update
```

---

# 11. Component 5 – Tone

Examples:

* Formal
* Respectful
* Professional
* Firm
* Persuasive
* Polite
* Appreciative

For a complaint:

> Firm but respectful.

For a job application:

> Professional and confident without exaggeration.

---

# 12. Component 6 – Format

Specify the required letter format.

For example:

```text
Output:
Sender's Address
Date
Recipient's Address
Subject
Salutation
Body
Closing
Name
```

This is especially important for academic assignments.

---

# 13. Component 7 – Constraints

Examples:

> Keep it below 300 words.

> Do not use aggressive language.

> Do not invent facts.

> Use simple formal English.

> Include only the information provided.

---

# 15. Example – Formal Complaint Letter

```text
Act as a professional business communication expert.

Write a formal complaint letter to the customer service
manager of an internet service provider.

Context:

Requirements:
- Clearly explain the problem.
- Mention the duration of the issue.
- Request immediate investigation.
- Request an update regarding resolution.
- Maintain a firm but respectful tone.
- Do not make unsupported accusations.
- Keep the letter below 300 words.

Output:
Sender Address
Date
Recipient Address
Subject
Salutation
Letter Body
Closing
```

# 22. Formal vs Informal Prompt

### Formal

```text
Write a formal letter to the college principal requesting
permission to organize a technical event.
Use respectful and professional language.
```

### Informal

```text
Write a friendly letter to my friend describing the technical
event I attended at college.
Use natural and conversational language.
```

The **purpose may be similar**, but the audience and relationship change the tone.

# 23. Letter Tone Transformation

Suppose the original statement is:

> Give me permission to conduct the event.

Prompt:

```text
Rewrite the following sentence for a formal permission letter.

Requirements:
- Maintain the original meaning.
- Use respectful language.
- Avoid sounding demanding.
```

Possible result:

> I kindly request your permission to organize the proposed event.

# 24. Improving a Poor Letter

### Poor Letter

> Sir, I want permission for event. We will conduct it next week. Please give permission quickly.

Problems:

* No subject
* No event details
* Informal language
* "Quickly" sounds demanding
* No proper structure
* No explanation of purpose

# 25. Improvement Prompt

```text
Act as a formal communication editor.

Improve the following letter.

Check:
- Structure
- Tone
- Grammar
- Clarity
- Completeness

Requirements:
- Preserve the original meaning.
- Do not invent event details.
- Identify missing information before rewriting.

Letter:
"""
[LETTER]
"""
```

An important addition is:

> **Identify missing information before rewriting.**

This can prevent AI from filling gaps with assumptions.

---

# 26. Ask AI for Missing Information

Instead of immediately generating the letter:

```text
Analyze my letter-writing request.

Before drafting the letter:
1. Identify information that is missing.
2. Explain why each missing detail matters.
3. Ask me for the minimum information required.

Do not draft the letter yet.
```

This is a powerful prompting practice.

---

# 27. Why This Is Better

### Traditional approach

```text
User
 ↓
"Write letter"
 ↓
AI guesses missing information
 ↓
Possible inaccuracies
```

### Better approach

```text
User
 ↓
Request
 ↓
AI identifies missing information
 ↓
User provides details
 ↓
AI drafts letter
 ↓
User reviews
```

This introduces **controlled prompting**.

# 32. Letter Generation with Multiple Versions

Sometimes students may want different levels of formality.

```text
Write three versions of the following letter:

1. Highly formal
2. Professional and concise
3. Simple formal English

Preserve the same facts and intended request in all versions.
Do not add information.
```

This helps students understand **tone control**.

# 33. Letter Prompt for Specific Audience

Compare:

> Write a letter requesting project information.

with:

> Write a formal letter to the project coordinator requesting information about the project's submission requirements for final-year engineering students.

The second prompt provides:

* Recipient
* Context
* Purpose
* Audience

Therefore, the output is likely to be more relevant.

# 34. Prompt for Letter Structure

Students can ask AI to provide the structure first.

```text
Create an outline for a formal complaint letter about
delayed delivery of a product.

Provide:
1. Opening
2. Problem description
3. Relevant details
4. Expected resolution
5. Closing

Do not write the full letter yet.
```

Then:

```text
Using the approved outline, write the complete letter.
```

# 38. Classroom Activity – Missing Information Challenge

Give students:

> Write a letter requesting permission for an event.

Ask AI:

> **Do not write the letter. First identify what information is missing.**

Students then provide the missing details.

This teaches an important principle:

> **A good prompt is not always about giving more instructions; sometimes it is about identifying what information is missing.**

# 42. Best Practices

### Do

* Clearly identify the recipient.
* Explain the purpose.
* Provide relevant background.
* Specify the desired tone.
* Mention important facts.
* Define the required format.
* Use placeholders for sensitive information.
* Ask AI to identify missing information when necessary.
* Review the final letter.
* Verify names, dates, addresses, and claims.

### Don't

* Ask AI to invent reasons.
* Exaggerate complaints.
* Add false qualifications.
* Provide unnecessary sensitive information.
* Assume AI knows local institutional formatting requirements.
* Submit generated letters without reviewing them.

---

# 43. Privacy and Sensitive Information

When creating letters with AI, students should avoid unnecessarily sharing:

* Government identification numbers
* Bank information
* Passwords
* Private medical details
* Confidential organizational information
* Personal information about other individuals

Use placeholders instead:

```text
[STUDENT NAME]
[ADDRESS]
[APPLICATION NUMBER]
[INSTITUTION NAME]
```

# 44. Common Mistakes

| Mistake                  | Better Approach                        |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| "Write a letter"         | Specify letter type                    |
| No recipient             | Identify recipient                     |
| No purpose               | Clearly state purpose                  |
| No context               | Give relevant background               |
| Wrong tone               | Define relationship and tone           |
| Missing format           | Specify structure                      |
| AI fills missing details | Ask AI to identify missing information |
| Fake information         | Explicitly prohibit fabrication        |
| Excessive length         | Specify approximate length             |
| No review                | Proofread and verify                   |

# Summary

Prompt Engineering can help students create **formal, informal, and semi-formal letters** more efficiently. The quality of the generated letter depends on how clearly the prompt communicates the **recipient, purpose, context, key information, tone, constraints, and required format**.

One particularly useful technique is to ask AI to **identify missing information before generating the letter**. This reduces the chance that the AI will make assumptions or introduce incorrect details.

Students should also remember that AI-generated communication must be reviewed before use. Names, dates, addresses, claims, reasons, and other factual information should always be checked.

> **Good letter prompting is not about making AI write more—it is about giving AI the right context to communicate accurately and appropriately.**
