# UNIT 2 – Fundamental of Prompt Writing

# Topic 10: Creating Prompts for Blog Writing

---

## Learning Objectives

After completing this topic, students will be able to:

- Understand the purpose and structure of a blog.
- Differentiate between a blog and other forms of writing.
- Create effective prompts for generating blog posts.
- Define audience, purpose, tone, structure, and length.
- Generate blog outlines before writing complete content.
- Create informative, educational, technical, and promotional blogs.
- Use AI to improve titles, introductions, headings, and readability.
- Review AI-generated blogs for accuracy, originality, and relevance.

---

# 1. Introduction

A **blog** is an online article designed to communicate information, ideas, opinions, experiences, or insights to a specific audience.

Blogs are commonly used for:

- Education
- Technology
- Business
- Marketing
- Personal experiences
- Tutorials
- Product information
- News and trends
- Professional knowledge sharing

Examples of blog topics:

- "What Is Generative AI?"
- "10 Tips for Learning C++"
- "How Students Can Use AI for Learning"
- "Introduction to Cloud Computing"
- "How to Build Your First Website"

---

# 2. Blog vs Essay

Students often confuse blogs with essays.

| Blog                                | Essay                              |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Usually written for online readers  | Usually academic or formal         |
| Reader-focused                      | Argument/analysis-focused          |
| Often uses headings and subheadings | May use continuous paragraphs      |
| Can be conversational               | Usually more formal                |
| May use lists and short sections    | Usually follows academic structure |
| Designed for easy online reading    | Designed for detailed discussion   |

The appropriate structure depends on the purpose and audience.

---

# 3. Why Prompt Engineering Matters for Blogs

Consider:

> Write a blog about AI.

This prompt is too broad.

AI does not know:

- Who will read it?
- What level should it target?
- What is the purpose?
- How long should it be?
- Should it be technical?
- Should it be conversational?
- What sections are required?

A better prompt provides these details.

---

# 5. Component 1 – Role

Define the perspective AI should take.

Examples:

> Act as a technology blogger.

> Act as an educational content writer.

> Act as a technical writer specializing in computer science.

> Act as a digital marketing content writer.

The role helps establish the intended writing approach.

---

# 6. Component 2 – Topic

Clearly define the subject.

### Weak

> Write about programming.

### Better

> Write a beginner-friendly blog explaining why learning Data Structures is important for software development students.

The second prompt defines both the topic and direction.

---

# 7. Component 3 – Target Audience

This is one of the most important components.

Examples:

- School students
- College students
- B.Tech IT students
- Beginners
- Software developers
- Teachers
- Business professionals
- General readers

Compare:

> Explain Prompt Engineering.

with:

> Explain Prompt Engineering to first-year engineering students who have basic knowledge of programming but no prior experience with Generative AI.

The second provides much better context.

---

# 8. Component 4 – Purpose

Define what the blog should achieve.

Examples:

- Educate
- Explain
- Inform
- Persuade
- Entertain
- Create awareness
- Provide a tutorial
- Promote a product/service

Example:

> Purpose: Educate beginners about the practical applications of Generative AI.

---

# 9. Component 5 – Key Points

Specify what must be covered.

Example:

```text id="j4x8p1"
Include:
- Definition of Generative AI
- How it differs from traditional AI
- Common applications
- Benefits
- Limitations
- Practical examples
- Future opportunities
```

This reduces the chance of missing important sections.

---

# 10. Component 6 – Tone

Possible tones include:

- Professional
- Conversational
- Educational
- Friendly
- Persuasive
- Inspirational
- Technical
- Informative

Example:

> Use a professional but beginner-friendly tone.

---

# 11. Component 7 – Structure

A blog can use:

```text id="q3m7v5"
Title
 ↓
Introduction
 ↓
Heading 1
 ↓
Heading 2
 ↓
Heading 3
 ↓
Examples
 ↓
Practical Tips
 ↓
Conclusion
```

For technical blogs, additional sections may include:

- Code examples
- Tables
- Diagrams
- Frequently Asked Questions
- References

---

# 12. Component 8 – Length

Avoid vague instructions such as:

> Write a detailed blog.

Instead:

> Write approximately 1,200–1,500 words.

or:

> Write a concise blog of approximately 700 words.

The requested length should match the topic and audience.

---

# 13. Component 9 – Constraints

Examples:

> Use simple English.

> Avoid unnecessary jargon.

> Include practical examples.

> Use short paragraphs.

> Do not invent statistics.

> Clearly distinguish facts from opinions.

# 18. Creating a Blog Outline First

Instead of immediately asking AI to write the complete article:

```text id="f4m7q2"
Create a detailed outline for a blog titled:

"10 Practical Uses of Generative AI for Students"

Audience:
Engineering students.

For each section provide:
- Heading
- Main idea
- Key points
- Suggested example

Do not write the complete blog yet.
```

---

# 19. Why Generate an Outline First?

### Without planning

```text
Topic
 ↓
AI writes
 ↓
Possible repetition
 ↓
Missing sections
 ↓
Weak conclusion
```

### With planning

```text
Topic
 ↓
Outline
 ↓
Review
 ↓
Modify
 ↓
Write Blog
 ↓
Review
```

The second method gives the writer more control.

# 22. Blog Introduction Prompt

The introduction should tell readers:

- What the topic is
- Why it matters
- What they will learn

Prompt:

```text id="x6q2m9"
Write an introduction for a blog titled:

"Why Every Computer Science Student Should Learn Prompt Engineering"

Audience:
Undergraduate CS/IT students.

Requirements:
- 100–150 words.
- Start with a relevant problem or observation.
- Clearly introduce the topic.
- Explain why it matters.
- Avoid exaggerated claims.
- End with a sentence explaining what the reader will learn.
```

---

# 23. Improving a Weak Introduction

### Weak

> AI is very popular nowadays. Prompt Engineering is also popular. In this blog we will discuss Prompt Engineering.

Problems:

- Generic opening
- Repetition
- No compelling context
- No clear value for the reader

Improved direction:

> Generative AI has changed how students search for information, write content, analyze problems, and learn technical concepts. But the quality of an AI response often depends on how effectively the user communicates the task.

The introduction immediately establishes context.

# 30. Blog Fact-Checking Prompt

AI-generated content may contain inaccurate information.

A useful prompt is:

```text id="v3k7n1"
Review the following blog for factual claims.

For each factual claim:
1. Identify the claim.
2. Classify it as:
   - Clearly supported
   - Needs verification
   - Potentially inaccurate
3. Explain what should be verified.

Do not invent sources or citations.
```

**Important:** AI's assessment is not itself proof that a claim is true. Important facts should be verified using reliable sources.

---

# 31. Blog Optimization

After creating a first draft:

```text id="q6m2r8"
Review this blog for reader engagement.

Identify:
- Weak opening
- Unnecessary sections
- Repeated ideas
- Long paragraphs
- Unclear headings
- Missing examples
- Weak conclusion

Then recommend the five highest-priority improvements.

Do not rewrite the blog yet.
```

This follows an important Prompt Engineering principle:

> **Diagnose first, then generate.**

---

# 32. Blog Revision

After reviewing:

```text id="k8p4v5"
Revise the blog based on the approved improvements.

Requirements:
- Preserve the factual meaning.
- Keep the same target audience.
- Maintain the professional tone.
- Improve transitions.
- Reduce repetition.
- Strengthen weak sections.
- Keep approximately the same length.
```

# 33. Blog Prompt Chaining

A complex blog can be developed in stages:

```text
Topic
  ↓
Audience
  ↓
Title Ideas
  ↓
Outline
  ↓
Section Development
  ↓
First Draft
  ↓

  ↓

  ↓
```

This is much more controllable than asking:

# 41. Best Practices

### Do

- Define the audience.
- Define the purpose.
- Specify the topic clearly.
- Provide a structure.
- Use practical examples.
- Request appropriate tone.
- Specify approximate length.
- Ask for an outline first for complex topics.
- Verify factual claims.
- Edit the final content.

### Don't

- Ask for "the best blog" without criteria.
- Use unsupported statistics.
- Treat AI-generated facts as automatically correct.
- Stuff keywords unnaturally.
- Copy content without reviewing it.
- Use excessive jargon for beginners.
- Make unsupported claims simply because AI generated them.

| Mistake                 | Better Approach                  |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------- |

| No audience             | Define target reader             |
| No purpose              | State intended outcome           |
| Generic title           | Request title options            |

| Very long paragraphs    | Request short paragraphs         |
| Repetition              | Ask for redundancy review        |
| Unsupported statistics  | Verify claims                    |

# Summary

Prompt Engineering makes blog creation more **structured, audience-focused, and controllable**. A strong blog prompt clearly defines **what to write, who it is for, why it is being written, what information it should contain, how it should sound, and how it should be structured**.

For high-quality blogs, students should avoid asking AI to produce the entire article immediately. A better workflow is to **define the audience and purpose → generate an outline → create the draft → review it → verify factual claims → refine the content**.

Most importantly, AI-generated blogs should not be treated as automatically accurate. Technical facts, statistics, dates, quotations, and other important claims should be independently verified.

> **A good blog prompt doesn't simply tell AI what to write—it tells AI who the reader is, why the content matters, and what the reader should take away.**
